🧲 Magnet

The magnet levitates because the superconductor expels magnetic fields (the Meissner effect), creating repulsion that balances gravity.

🧊 Superconductor

Below its critical temperature (Tc), this material conducts current with zero resistance and perfectly repels magnetic fields.

🌀 Flux Vortices

In Type II superconductors, magnetic flux penetrates in thin tubes called vortices. Each vortex carries one quantum of magnetic flux.

About Superconductivity

Superconductors are materials that, when cooled below a certain temperature, conduct electricity without resistance and expel magnetic fields. This phenomenon was discovered by Heike Kamerlingh Onnes in 1911.

Type I vs Type II

  • Type I: Pure metals (Pb, Sn, Hg). Expel all magnetic fields completely; lose superconductivity abruptly above Hc.
  • Type II: Alloys/oxides (Nb₃Sn, YBCO). Allow partial magnetic flux through quantized vortices; superconductivity lasts up to Hc2.

Applications

  • Maglev trains (levitation)
  • MRI machines
  • Quantum computers
  • Particle accelerators
  • Lossless power transmission
